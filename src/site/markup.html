How it works

Three steps. Then help can start.

  1. 01

    Carry

    Wallet card or a sticker on a phone, helmet, or bag. It stays with you.

  2. 02

    Tap to scan

    A responder taps with any NFC phone. No app, no mobile data, no Wi-Fi.

  3. 03

    Help starts

    Name, blood type, ICE contact, and allergies appear as plain text.

The problem

First responders should not have to guess.

In an emergency the person who needs care often cannot speak. Allergies, blood type, and who to call are the details that change the first minutes — and they are useless if they live only in a phone that is locked.

LifeTag writes those few lines onto the chip itself. The phone only reads what is already there.

After you submit

The chip is written once, by hand.

There is no cloud profile to edit later. You check the text, we encode that exact text, then test the tag in airplane mode before it leaves 12-B Alcalde Jose, Pasig.

  1. 1 You choose Lite, Pro, or Duo.
  2. 2 You enter only emergency fields.
  3. 3 You confirm the text that will be burned in.
  4. 4 A phone tap shows it, online or not.

Privacy

Only what a stranger needs in the first minute.

  • Collected: name, blood type, one ICE number, allergies or conditions, and an optional care note.
  • Not collected: address, government ID, medical history, photos, or accounts.
  • Stored on the chip as static NDEF text. This demo keeps a copy in your browser only — it is not sent to a server.

LifeTag is a student venture prototype from Pamantasan ng Lungsod ng Pasig, College of Computer Studies. It is a memory aid for responders, not a medical record and not a certified device.
